# PMP Glossary

174 PMP terms and services, each in one plain-English sentence.

## #

- **100% rule**: The WBS contains all in-scope work and nothing else, and each level's children sum to the parent.

## A

- **ADKAR**: A change model of awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement for individual adoption.
- **Agile suitability filter**: A tool that scores culture, team and project to judge how well agile delivery fits.
- **Alternative dispute resolution (ADR)**: Settling a contract dispute through mediation or arbitration rather than litigation.
- **Anchoring**: The tendency of a group to converge on the first or most senior view it hears.

## B

- **BATNA**: The best alternative to a negotiated agreement, which sets a realistic walk-away point.
- **Benefits management plan**: The plan setting out each benefit's metric, baseline, target, timing, owner, assumptions and risks.
- **Bridges' transition model**: A model of the emotional transition through ending, the neutral zone and a new beginning.
- **Budget at completion (BAC)**: The total value of the cost baseline.
- **Burn rate**: The cost of an agile team per iteration or per month.

## C

- **Cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagram**: A diagram that sorts many possible causes of a problem into categories.
- **Ceiling price**: The most a buyer pays under a fixed price incentive fee contract.
- **Change control board (CCB)**: The group authorized to approve, reject or defer changes above the project manager's delegated limits.
- **Change log**: The register of change requests and their decisions.
- **Change management plan**: The plan component defining what counts as a change, who approves which changes, thresholds, emergency procedures and tools.
- **Claim**: A formal assertion by one party that it is owed compensation or a change under a contract.
- **Communication requirements analysis**: Finding out what information each stakeholder group needs, why, how often and from whom before choosing methods.
- **Communications management plan**: The plan component recording how project communication will be planned, structured, delivered, monitored and escalated.
- **Community of practice**: A voluntary, practitioner-led network of people who share a subject across projects.
- **Compliance register (matrix)**: A record linking each obligation to its control, owner and evidence.
- **Configuration management**: Controlling the versions of the product and its documents so everyone works from the current approved version.
- **Configuration status accounting**: Recording the current version of each configuration item and which approved changes it contains.
- **Constructive change**: Extra work a buyer informally directs a seller to perform outside the contract.
- **Contingency plan**: A planned response carried out when a risk's trigger occurs.
- **Contingency reserve**: Budget inside the cost baseline for identified risks.
- **Control account**: A management control point that integrates scope, budget and schedule for one or more work packages.
- **Control chart**: A chart of a process over time against control limits that shows whether the process is stable.
- **Controlling PMO**: A PMO that requires compliance with its frameworks and audits projects for it.
- **Corrective action**: A change that brings current project performance back in line with the plan.
- **Cost baseline**: The approved, time-phased total of work package estimates plus contingency reserve; equal to BAC.
- **Cost of delay**: The value lost for each unit of time a piece of work is not delivered.
- **Cost of quality (CoQ)**: The total cost of prevention, appraisal and internal and external failure.
- **Crashing**: Adding resources to shorten critical activities at extra cost.
- **Critical path**: The longest path through the schedule network, which sets the shortest possible duration.
- **Cumulative flow diagram (CFD)**: A stacked chart of items in each workflow state over time.

## D

- **Defect repair**: A change that modifies a nonconforming product or component.
- **Definition of done**: The shared quality standard every item must meet before it counts as complete.
- **Definition of ready**: The entry condition a backlog item must meet before it is pulled into an iteration.
- **Delegation board**: A tool that records, per decision area, how much authority sits with the team.
- **Development approach**: The plan component recording whether work is run predictively, adaptively or as a hybrid, and how it is tailored.
- **Directions of influence**: A classification of stakeholders as upward, downward, outward or sideward relative to the project.
- **Directive PMO**: A PMO that directly manages projects, with project managers assigned by and reporting to it.

## E

- **Earned schedule**: A technique that converts earned value into time to give a date-based schedule performance index.
- **Emergency change**: A change the plan allows to be approved immediately under defined criteria, then documented and reviewed.
- **Emotional intelligence**: The ability to recognize and manage your own emotions and to read and influence those of others.
- **Enacted culture**: What people in the organization actually do and how that behaviour is rewarded or punished.
- **Engagement assessment matrix**: A table comparing each stakeholder's current and desired engagement level.
- **Enterprise environmental factors (EEFs)**: Conditions inside or outside the organization that influence the project but that the team does not control.
- **Epic**: A large body of work spanning several iterations that is split into stories later.
- **Escalation path**: The agreed route, with owners and response times at each level, that a decision follows when it exceeds a level's authority.
- **Escalation threshold (tolerance)**: The agreed limit of variance beyond which a decision moves to a higher authority.
- **Espoused culture**: What an organization says it values.
- **Estimate at completion (EAC)**: The forecast total cost of the project.
- **Expected monetary value (EMV)**: Probability multiplied by impact, summed across outcomes.
- **Explicit knowledge**: Knowledge that can be written down and used by someone else without its author present.

## F

- **Fallback plan**: A second planned response used if the contingency plan is ineffective.
- **Fast tracking**: Overlapping activities planned in sequence, at extra risk.
- **Final project report**: The closure document summarizing performance against scope, schedule, cost, quality and value.
- **Fist of five**: A quick consensus check in which each person shows zero to five fingers to signal support for a proposal.
- **Five whys**: A root-cause technique that follows one chain of causes by asking why repeatedly.
- **Free float**: How long an activity can slip without delaying the early start of any successor.
- **Funding limit reconciliation**: Comparing planned spend per period with the funding released in that period and adjusting the schedule.

## G

- **Gold plating**: Work the team adds that the customer did not request.
- **Grade**: The category or feature level of a deliverable, distinct from its quality.
- **Ground rules**: Agreed expectations for how team members behave and work together.
- **Guardrails**: Limits set by governance within which agile teams decide freely, with only exceptions reviewed.

## H

- **Hybrid approach**: Predictive methods for well-defined components and adaptive methods for uncertain ones, integrated through shared milestones and governance.
- **Hypercare**: A time-boxed period of extra project support after go-live, with exit criteria.

## I

- **Impact analysis**: The assessment of everything a proposed change would affect before it is decided.
- **Impediment**: Anything that stops or slows the team from doing its work.
- **Incremental life cycle**: A life cycle that delivers usable pieces frequently, each built to known requirements.
- **Information radiator**: A large, highly visible display of the team's real work status.
- **Integrated project management plan**: The single approved plan combining every subsidiary plan, the baselines and the delivery approach.
- **INVEST**: The qualities of a good story: independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, small and testable.
- **Issue**: A current condition or problem that is affecting the project's objectives and must be resolved.
- **Issue log**: The project document where current problems and agreed resolutions are recorded with owners and dates.
- **Iterative life cycle**: A life cycle that refines the solution through repeated feedback rounds but delivers once.

## K

- **Kaizen**: Continuous, incremental improvement in which everyone contributes ideas.
- **Kano model**: A model classifying features as basic, performance, excitement or indifferent by their effect on satisfaction.
- **Kotter's 8-step process**: A change model for leaders, from creating urgency to anchoring the change in the culture.

## L

- **Lessons learned register**: The project document where the team records lessons as they emerge.
- **Lessons learned repository**: The organization's store of lessons from past projects, an organizational process asset.
- **Level of effort**: An earned value method for support work in which earned value equals planned value.
- **Little's Law**: Average cycle time equals average work in progress divided by average throughput.

## M

- **Make-or-buy analysis**: A comparison of doing work in-house with buying it, counting cost, risk, capability and control.
- **Management reserve**: Budget outside the cost baseline for unidentified work within scope.
- **Minimum marketable feature (MMF)**: The smallest piece of functionality with standalone value to customers.
- **Minimum viable product (MVP)**: The smallest working release that lets the team learn whether customers value something.
- **Monte Carlo simulation**: A model run many times over ranges of inputs to produce a probability distribution of outcomes.
- **MoSCoW**: A prioritization scheme sorting items into must have, should have, could have and won't have this time.
- **Multicriteria decision analysis (MCDA)**: A decision matrix that scores options against agreed, weighted criteria.

## N

- **Net present value (NPV)**: The value an investment creates in today's money after discounting future cash flows.
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**: The percentage of promoters (scores 9–10) minus the percentage of detractors (0–6) on a 0–10 likelihood-to-recommend scale.
- **Noise**: Anything that distorts a message between sender and receiver, such as jargon, poor connections or cultural differences.
- **Nominal group technique**: A method that collects silent individual ideas before group discussion and ranking.

## O

- **Organizational process assets (OPAs)**: The plans, processes, policies, templates and knowledge bases the organization owns and can update.
- **Osmotic communication**: Useful information absorbed by overhearing conversations in a shared team space.
- **Overall project risk**: The effect of all uncertainty on the project as a whole.

## P

- **Payback period**: The time it takes to recover an investment.
- **PDCA**: The plan-do-check-act improvement cycle.
- **Performance measurement baseline (PMB)**: The integrated scope, schedule and cost baselines against which earned value is measured.
- **PESTLE**: A scan of political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors.
- **Phase gate**: A governance review at the end of a phase that decides whether the project continues, changes or stops.
- **Planning package**: Budgeted work below a control account that is known but not yet decomposed into work packages.
- **Power/interest grid**: A four-quadrant model placing stakeholders by power and interest to choose an engagement strategy.
- **Predictive life cycle**: A life cycle with requirements fixed up front and a single delivery at the end.
- **Preventive action**: A change that keeps future performance in line with the plan.
- **Privity of contract**: The principle that a buyer's contract is with the seller, not the seller's subcontractors.
- **Procurement statement of work (SOW)**: The description of the work being bought, detailed enough for sellers to price it.
- **Product box**: A facilitation exercise in which stakeholders design the product's retail box, forcing agreement on its few most important benefits.
- **Product roadmap**: A view of how the vision unfolds over time through themes, releases and the timing of benefits.
- **Product vision statement**: A few sentences saying who the product is for, which need it meets and how it differs from alternatives.
- **Progressive elaboration**: Adding detail to plans as more becomes known.
- **Project charter**: The document that records the project's purpose, measurable objectives and success criteria and formally authorizes the project and the project manager.
- **Project governance**: The framework of structure, decision rights, rules, procedures, reporting, ethics and policies that directs and oversees a project.
- **Project scope statement**: The document describing product scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria and exclusions.
- **Project vision**: A short, shared statement of the outcome a project must deliver and why it matters, used to judge every trade-off.
- **Psychological safety**: A shared belief that it is safe to speak up, admit mistakes and raise risks without blame.

## Q

- **Quality audit**: An independent review of whether project activities follow the agreed policies and procedures.
- **Quality metric**: A measurable attribute with a target and tolerance that shows whether quality is being met.

## R

- **RACI chart**: A responsibility assignment matrix showing who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for each activity.
- **Requirements traceability matrix**: A table linking each requirement to its source, the deliverables that meet it and the tests that verify it.
- **Residual risk**: The risk that remains after a response has been carried out.
- **Resource breakdown structure (RBS)**: A hierarchy of resources grouped by category and type.
- **Resource calendar**: A record of when a specific resource is available to work.
- **Resource histogram**: A bar chart of planned load per period against available capacity.
- **Resource leveling**: Moving activity dates to respect resource limits, which may extend the finish date.
- **Resource management plan**: The plan describing how team and physical resources are identified, acquired, developed, managed, recognized and released.
- **Resource smoothing**: Moving activities within their float to keep resource demand under a limit without changing the finish date.
- **Risk appetite**: How much uncertainty the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.
- **Risk audit**: A review of whether the risk management process is working.
- **Risk breakdown structure (RBS)**: A hierarchy of risk categories used to prompt identification.
- **Risk data quality assessment**: A check of how accurate, reliable and complete the information behind a risk rating is.
- **Risk management plan**: The plan describing how risk will be identified, analyzed, responded to, monitored and reported.
- **Risk register**: The record of each individual risk with its owner, ratings, triggers, responses and status.
- **Risk threshold**: A measurable level of risk exposure set from appetite, above which a risk needs an active response.
- **Rolling wave planning**: Detailing near-term work now and keeping later work at a higher level until it approaches.
- **Rule of seven**: Seven consecutive control-chart points on one side of the mean, signalling a likely process shift.

## S

- **Salience model**: A model classifying stakeholders by power, legitimacy and urgency.
- **Satir change model**: A model showing performance dropping into chaos after a change, then recovering to a new status quo.
- **Schedule baseline**: The approved schedule used as the reference for measuring schedule variance.
- **Scope baseline**: The approved project scope statement, WBS and WBS dictionary.
- **Scope creep**: Uncontrolled additions to scope requested by stakeholders without approval.
- **Secondary risk**: A new risk created by implementing a risk response.
- **Servant leadership**: A leadership stance that puts the team's needs first by removing obstacles, coaching and enabling rather than commanding.
- **Situational leadership**: A model that matches the leader's mix of direction and support to a follower's competence and commitment on a task.
- **Speed Leas' levels of conflict**: A five-level escalation model running from problem to solve, disagreement, contest and crusade to world war.
- **Spike**: A time-boxed investigation that produces knowledge rather than a product increment.
- **Stakeholder register**: The project document recording each stakeholder's identification, assessment and classification.
- **Steering committee**: A group of senior stakeholders that takes decisions beyond individual authority and oversees the project.
- **Success metrics**: Agreed outcome measures, traced to the business case, that show whether the project achieved what it was approved to achieve.
- **Sunk cost**: Money already spent, which is excluded from decisions about whether to continue.
- **Supportive PMO**: A PMO that offers templates, practices and training on request without requiring their use.
- **Swarming**: The team working together on one blocked item, with the specialist steering.

## T

- **Tacit knowledge**: Experience, judgement and know-how that is hard to write down and moves mainly through interaction.
- **Tailoring**: Adapting the organization's processes to a project within the limits the methodology allows.
- **Team charter (working agreement)**: The team's own record of its values, ground rules and working norms, created by the team.
- **Three-point estimate**: An estimate built from optimistic, most likely and pessimistic values, using a beta (PERT) or triangular average.
- **Throughput**: The number of items a team finishes per unit of time.
- **To-complete performance index (TCPI)**: The cost efficiency the remaining work must achieve to meet a cost target.
- **Tornado diagram**: A sensitivity chart ranking which uncertainties most affect the result.
- **Total float**: How long an activity can slip without delaying the project finish.
- **Trigger**: A warning sign that a risk is about to occur or has become more likely.
- **Tuckman's stages of team development**: The team development stages of forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning.

## U

- **User story**: A small, valuable piece of user-visible behaviour sized to finish within one iteration.

## V

- **Value stream mapping**: A lean technique that maps every step from request to delivery to separate value-adding time from waiting.
- **Voluntary standard**: An industry standard an organization adopts by choice, which becomes binding once written into a contract.

## W

- **Watch list**: Low-priority risks kept in the register and re-rated at reviews.
- **Watermelon report**: A status report that shows green on the outside while the project is red underneath.
- **WBS dictionary**: The document giving the detailed description, owner, cost, milestones and acceptance criteria of each WBS component.
- **Weighted shortest job first (WSJF)**: A ranking of work by cost of delay divided by duration or size.
- **Work breakdown structure (WBS)**: A deliverable-oriented hierarchy decomposing total scope down to work packages.
- **Work package**: The lowest WBS level, small enough to estimate, assign and monitor.
- **Workaround**: An unplanned response to a problem or risk that was not planned for and has occurred.
