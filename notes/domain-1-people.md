# Domain 1: People (33%)

This domain covers leading and engaging people: a shared vision, conflict, team leadership, stakeholders, knowledge transfer and communication. Almost every question is a short predictive, agile or hybrid scenario that asks what the project manager should do, do first or do next. The answer that wins usually understands the situation before acting, involves the team or the people affected before deciding for them, and works through the agreed process and the right person before escalating or going around them.

## 1.1 Develop a common vision

- **Vision = the why; scope = the what.** The vision is a short, shared statement of the outcome and the value it creates. Scope (scope baseline in predictive work, backlog in agile work) defines what is delivered. Every trade-off is judged against the vision, even on a fixed-price project with an approved baseline.
- **Four enablers:** help ensure a shared vision with key stakeholders, promote it, keep it current, and break down misunderstandings to their root cause.

| Artifact or technique               | What it does                                                                 | Use it when                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Project charter                     | Purpose, measurable objectives, success criteria; **authorizes** the project | The formal record of why the project exists       |
| Scope statement                     | Deliverables, acceptance criteria, exclusions; does **not** authorize        | Defining what is built                            |
| Product vision (elevator) statement | Who it is for, what need it meets, how it differs                            | A lightweight vision anyone can repeat            |
| Product box                         | Imagined retail box with room for only a few benefits                        | Forcing agreement on the few benefits that matter |
| Product roadmap                     | Themes, releases, when benefits arrive                                       | The gap is **when** value arrives, not why        |

- **Co-create it.** A single-author draft gives awareness, not commitment. Facilitate refinement with the people who must live by it; distance changes the logistics, not the need.
- **Counter anchoring:** capture individual views before anyone hears the senior voice (brainwriting, nominal group technique).
- **Competing visions** go to the sponsor early, before the charter is signed or design locks one in. Delegating the choice to one party, letting each pursue its own version, or deferring all fail.
- **Ownership:**
  - Sponsor: business case, chartered objectives, and any change to them.
  - Product owner: the product goal and backlog order (Scrum Guide 2020).
  - Project manager: facilitates, promotes, coaches. If the product owner is weak, coach them; do not add a second owner.
- **Promote it in decisions,** not by repeating it: information radiators, iteration goals framed around the vision, work packages traced to objectives, and asking the team how their work advances it.
- **Newcomers:** re-establish the vision with them, sized to the gap. A few new team members get a direct walkthrough; a new key stakeholder gets early two-way engagement; heavy turnover on a long program means re-establishing it with the current group.
- **Keep it current** when the purpose is in question (strategy, merger, market, regulation, user evidence). Schedule slips and cost variance are planning problems, not vision problems. Revisit the vision first, then let the plan follow.
- **Who approves a change:** anything touching chartered objectives, budget or timeline goes to the sponsor. Refinements inside them are the product owner's call.
- **Hybrid:** every stream reports against one shared outcome, with joint reviews at integration points and cross-stream trade-offs resolved jointly.
- **Diagnose before fixing.** Symptoms: different backlog rankings, people who cannot link their work to the outcome, deliverables that meet criteria but miss the point. Compare interpretations first, then use a fishbone (categories of causes) or five whys (one causal chain). Fix the source, such as a channel repeating the wrong message.

**Traps**

- An iteration goal covers one iteration, not the product. A burnup chart shows delivery progress, not when benefits arrive.
- Restating the vision does not fix a timing gap; a roadmap does.
- An unofficial "the aim has changed" claim is confirmed with the sponsor before the team acts on it.

## 1.2 Manage conflicts

- **Six enablers:** identify the source, analyze the context, implement an agreed resolution, communicate conflict principles, foster adherence to ground rules, and rectify violations.
- **Find the real source.** A "personality clash" often hides a resource, priority or role conflict. Check the resource calendar or RACI before choosing a response.
  - In a matrix, conflict over a shared person's time is often structural: agree priorities with whoever controls that person's time.
- **Context questions:** importance, urgency, relationships, authority and power, culture and channel, and level of escalation. Move disputes out of public or written channels into direct conversation.

| Technique                   | Fits when                                                         | Cost                                                |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Collaborate / problem solve | Time available, parties open, lasting commitment matters          | Needs time and trust                                |
| Compromise / reconcile      | Equal power, short time, a workable split is enough               | Settles positions, not interests; disputes return   |
| Force / direct              | Emergency, safety or legal non-negotiable, immediate deadline     | Leaves a loser; repair the relationship afterwards  |
| Smooth / accommodate        | Low-stakes issue, relationship matters more                       | Issue stays open                                    |
| Withdraw / avoid            | Tempers need to cool, or trivial issue, and the decision can wait | Never a fix; postponing important issues grows them |

- **Speed Leas' five levels** (intervene more directly as the level rises):
  1. Problem to solve: open sharing. Let the team solve it.
  2. Disagreement: vague language, self-protection. Support the team; bring facts into the open.
  3. Contest: taking sides, keeping score. Facilitated negotiation.
  4. Crusade: protecting one's group, refusing to meet. Diplomacy, working with each side separately.
  5. World war: intent to harm. Prevent harm, including separating the parties.
- **Servant leadership:** a self-organizing team gets the first chance. Facilitate when a disagreement stalls and threatens the sprint goal; step in directly when it turns personal and the team's own attempt failed. Escalate only after your own attempt.
- **After agreement:** record it in the **issue log** (not the risk register), respect it, follow up, and close it only once it holds. If it breaks down, meet the parties to learn why.
- **Communicate the conflict approach up front** to the team (team charter, at kickoff and when membership changes) and to external parties (agree the escalation path with each organization's lead).
- **Ground rules** are written by the team, facilitated by the PM, recorded in the team charter. Revisit them with the whole team when members join.
- **Violations:** raise privately and early with the person, referring to the agreement; understand why; let the team hold itself accountable; escalate (in a matrix, to the functional manager) only after that fails.

**Traps**

- Splitting the difference is compromise, not collaboration.
- A safety or legal requirement is not negotiable: direct the outcome rather than compromising or routing it through change control.
- A stricter new rule does not fix one person ignoring an existing one.

## 1.3 Lead the project team

- **Seven enablers:** set team expectations, empower the team, solve problems, represent the team, support varied perspectives, choose a leadership style, and clarify roles.
- **Team charter / working agreement:** written by the team, facilitated by the PM. Holds values, ground rules, core hours, the definition of done, and how decisions and disagreements are handled. It is a living document; a rule stands until the team changes it.
- **When someone misses a norm:** talk privately to understand the cause, clarify expectations, agree a specific improvement plan, and escalate only if that plan fails.
- **Empowerment:** the team decides how to do the work and who takes which item. Stakeholders get visibility (board, forecast), not control over assignments.
  - In predictive work, delegate activity planning inside work packages; the baselines stay under change control.
  - Make **decision rights explicit** (for example a delegation board). Governance-mandated approvals stay with the named authority.
  - If a delegated limit is crossed, find out why with the team before pulling authority back.
- **Servant leadership** is PMI's default: remove obstacles, coach, step out of the centre.

| Follower on this task                         | Situational style | Direction / support |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------- |
| Low competence, high commitment               | Directing         | High / low          |
| Some competence, low commitment               | Coaching          | High / high         |
| Moderate–high competence, variable commitment | Supporting        | Low / high          |
| High competence, high commitment              | Delegating        | Low / low           |

- **Tuckman:** forming (direct), storming (coach), norming (support), performing (delegate), adjourning (recognize, release). New members or new work can send a performing team back to storming; involve the whole team in revisiting how it works.
- **Emotional intelligence:** self-awareness (recognize your own reaction), self-management (control it), social awareness (read others), relationship management (act on that to influence and build trust).
- **Problem solving:** gather data, facilitate root-cause analysis with the team (five whys, fishbone, Pareto), rank causes by data, trial a fix, re-check.
- **Represent the team:** take its evidence to whoever owns the constraint. Discuss mid-iteration pull-aways with the functional manager; give a sponsor who wants an earlier date the estimates and options; take a burdensome process to its owner with an alternative.
- **Inclusion and psychological safety:** change the format, not the people (round-robin, silent brainstorming, written input, asynchronous decision windows). Run blameless reviews and thank people for early bad news.
- **RACI:** exactly one **A** per activity; many C's stall decisions. Agile: product owner orders the backlog; developers plan the sprint and judge how much fits; Scrum Master coaches and removes impediments.

**Traps**

- Penalising someone before you know the cause punishes before it diagnoses.
- Style follows the person's competence and commitment on this task, not their seniority.
- Anyone other than the product owner reordering the backlog gives developers conflicting priorities.

## 1.4 Engage stakeholders

- **Loop:** identify → analyze → plan engagement → execute → monitor and reassess. Most questions ask which step the project is at and whether an option skips one.
- **Identify early and continuously,** as soon as the charter exists. Sources: charter and business documents, existing stakeholders (snowball), lessons learned, experts, org charts, contracts, regulations.
- A newly found stakeholder is **recorded and analyzed first**. If they control something imminent (a release, a permit), engage them before it.

| Power / interest | Strategy       | In practice                                    |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| High / high      | Manage closely | Involve in decisions; frequent two-way contact |
| High / low       | Keep satisfied | Concise updates at their decision points       |
| Low / high       | Keep informed  | Regular information plus a feedback channel    |
| Low / low        | Monitor        | Minimal effort; watch for change               |

- Formal power is not the whole story: re-rate informal influence (an opinion leader) or leverage gained through a powerful body.
- **Reassess** when a stakeholder's role, power or behaviour changes, or a successor arrives. A successor is assessed afresh, not given the predecessor's rating.
- **Engagement assessment matrix:** current (C) vs desired (D) on five levels: unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading. Prioritize by gap size and how soon engagement is needed.
- **Stakeholder register:** identity, assessment and classification. Keep sensitive judgements in a restricted record.
- **Tailor communication:** push for targeted summaries, pull for large audiences, interactive to agree something together.
- **When engagement actions fail,** ask the stakeholder why and change the method. Resistant stakeholder: a direct, private conversation to understand the concern first.
- **Agile:** stakeholders engage mainly at the iteration review. If a key person cannot attend, adapt the format so they still inspect the product.
- **Alignment of needs:** the criterion is objective value; the decision owner is the sponsor (predictive) or product owner (agile). A stakeholder asking developers directly is redirected to the product owner.
- **Trust and influence:** share bad news early with options, never conceal defects, forecast as ranges (120 points at velocity 24–30 is about 4–5 iterations), address the specific fear with evidence, and escalate only after influence has been tried.

**Traps**

- "Keep informed" does not transfer decision rights.
- Supportive (favours the work) and leading (actively champions it) are different levels.

## 1.5 Align stakeholder expectations

- **Four enablers in order:** categorize stakeholders, identify expectations, facilitate discussions to align them, and organize and act on mentoring opportunities.
- **Salience model:** power, legitimacy, urgency. Check each attribute separately.

| Attributes           | Class         |
| -------------------- | ------------- |
| Power only           | Dormant       |
| Legitimacy only      | Discretionary |
| Urgency only         | Demanding     |
| Power + legitimacy   | Dominant      |
| Power + urgency      | Dangerous     |
| Legitimacy + urgency | Dependent     |
| All three            | Definitive    |

- **Directions of influence:** upward (sponsor, senior management, customer organization, steering committee), downward (team and contributing specialists), outward (suppliers, end users, regulators, the public), sideward (peer managers, aligned by negotiation between equals). Classify by relationship to the project, not home department.
- **Group** large populations by shared expectations; **split** a group whose expectations diverge; **re-categorize** whenever power, interest or expectations change.
- **Surface unstated expectations** with the people who hold them: interviews, vision workshops, prototypes and demos, observation of real work. Documents only hold stated ones.
- **Make expectations measurable** ("reliable" → an availability target) and record them as acceptance criteria. Agile: the product owner owns acceptance criteria. Predictive: record them in the requirements so they flow into the scope baseline.
- **Late expectations** (after the baseline): clarify, then change control. Before a baseline exists, align through facilitation.
- **Facilitate:** understand each party's interests separately, then run a joint session anchored on the shared objective. Round-robin and silent idea generation stop the loudest voice winning.
- **Decision techniques:** fist of five (consensus check on one proposal), MCDA (options scored against agreed weighted criteria), unanimity, majority (>50%), plurality (largest block), autocratic.
- **Agile:** MoSCoW makes trade-offs visible; the product owner makes the final ordering call. Align predictive-minded stakeholders with a backlog, a narrowing velocity-based date range, burnup charts and reviews.
- **Escalate** only after facilitation fails, the decision is beyond your authority, and delay is hurting the project; bring options and impacts.
- **Mentoring vs training:** mentoring closes a gap in role judgement and experience; training closes a gap in specific knowledge.

**Traps**

- Plurality is not majority. Under a >50% rule, the leader still under half has not won; run a run-off.
- Planning poker (sizing), Delphi (anonymous experts), Kano (satisfaction) and Pareto (frequency) are not group decision techniques.
- Placing a worried stakeholder in a higher-power quadrant promises influence the project cannot deliver.

## 1.6 Manage stakeholder expectations

- **Three enablers:** identify internal and external customer expectations, align and maintain outcomes to them, and monitor satisfaction and respond.
- **External customer:** outside the organization, receives or uses the outcome. **Internal customer:** inside it, operates, supports or works with the deliverable. Internal readiness work (procedures, training) is planned like any requirement.
- **Go to real users, not proxies** (sponsor, sales, market reports). Interviews capture what people say; observation captures what they do. If a contract names one point of contact, work through that contact to reach users.

| Kano category | Stated? | Present       | Missing                |
| ------------- | ------- | ------------- | ---------------------- |
| Basic         | Rarely  | No extra gain | Strong dissatisfaction |
| Performance   | Yes     | Rises with it | Proportional drop      |
| Excitement    | No      | Delight       | Not missed             |
| Indifferent   | No      | No effect     | No effect              |

- Delighters decay into basics over time; revalidate old classifications with current customers.
- **Outcomes over outputs:** judge alignment by the benefit in the business case, not features shipped or velocity. If the outcome is missing, investigate with customers and review with sponsor and product owner.
- **MVP and increments** test uncertain expectations with real behaviour. On hybrid work, plan stable deliverables predictively and uncertain ones incrementally.
- **Changes:** baselined work → impact analysis and the change control board; agile → the product owner and backlog. After a rejection, explain the decision and remaining options to the customer.
- **Gold plating:** the team adding unrequested work. Offer ideas through change control instead.
- **Control quality** verifies (built correctly); **validate scope** gets documented customer acceptance. The **requirements traceability matrix** links each requirement to its source, deliverables and tests.
- **Monitor satisfaction** with customer evidence: review feedback, NPS (% promoters 9–10 minus % detractors 0–6), CSAT, CES, usage, retention. Report per customer group so an average does not hide a struggling group.
- **Respond:** diagnose the specific cause with the affected group, then fix through the agreed route. Reset expectations early, after estimating with the team, and share forecasts when they move.

**Traps**

- Velocity, burndown and SPI/CPI describe delivery, not satisfaction.
- Informal approval is not acceptance.

## 1.7 Help ensure knowledge transfer

- **Three enablers:** identify critical knowledge, gather it, and foster an environment for transfer.

|           | Explicit                                  | Tacit                                                |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| What      | Codified facts, rules, artifacts          | Experience, judgement, skill, relationships          |
| Cue       | It already exists in a document or system | The holder "just knows" and cannot say how           |
| Moves via | Repositories, wikis, documents, training  | Shadowing, pairing, coaching, practice with feedback |

- Most knowledge is a mix: file the explicit part, shadow and introduce for the tacit part.
- **Critical knowledge** ranks by impact, concentration (how few hold it), exposure (is it written anywhere) and time to reacquire. Interview the people who do the work, not only their managers.
- **Key-person dependency is a risk.** Record it in the risk register and respond now, inside the team, typically by pairing a backup on upcoming work. On agile teams, make the risk visible to the product owner and plan the pairing into capacity with the team.
- **Gathering techniques:** observation and shadowing (practice differs from the manual), interviews, facilitated workshops (several experts, one agreed method), walkthroughs and storytelling, practice on past cases with expert critique (expert leaving before the next live cycle).
- **Capture as you go:** decision logs with rationale, documentation in the definition of done, runbook updates inside the story, as-built markups at the time of the field change.
- **Vendors:** make knowledge transfer a contracted deliverable with acceptance criteria. Missing clause → negotiate a contract change. Criteria unmet → do not accept.
- **Operations handover:** the receiving team learns by doing before go-live (testing, deployment rehearsals, time in the delivery team).
- **Environment:** psychological safety comes first. Address hoarding through its cause (job insecurity, incentives that reward heroics); work with functional managers when the incentive is structural.
- **Everyday practices:** pairing with rotation, swarming, rotating ownership (T-shaped skills), pairing-based onboarding, overlap hours for live work in distributed teams, shared decision records, and communities of practice (support without control).

**Traps**

- A document or course cannot carry a skill the expert cannot articulate.
- End-of-project lessons-learned workshops recover only what people remember.
- Keeping the project team on after go-live postpones transfer rather than achieving it.

## 1.8 Plan and manage communication

- **Six enablers:** define a strategy, promote transparency and collaboration, establish a feedback loop, understand reporting requirements, create aligned reports, and support governance.
- **Start with communication requirements analysis:** what each group needs, why, format, frequency, from whom, confidentiality. Repeat it when stakeholders or cadence change, and update the plan.
- **Channels = n(n − 1) ÷ 2**, counting the project manager. For a change, subtract totals: 5 → 7 people is 21 − 10 = **11 added**; 12 → 9 is 66 − 36 = **30 removed**.

| Method      | Examples                   | Best for                                        | Limit                         |
| ----------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Interactive | Meetings, calls, workshops | Urgent, complex or sensitive matters; agreement | Needs people at the same time |
| Push        | Email, memos, sent reports | Specific recipients who must receive it         | Confirms sending, not reading |
| Pull        | Wiki, portal, repository   | Large volumes or audiences who look things up   | People must choose to look    |

- Need understanding **and** a record (regulated work)? Combine a live session with written minutes.
- **Technology** is chosen by urgency, availability and reliability at each site, ease of use, environment (time zones, language), and sensitivity.
- **Communications management plan:** requirements, content, reason, frequency, sender, who may release confidential information, recipients, methods, resources, **escalation process**, how the plan is updated, glossary.
- **Sender-receiver model:** encode → medium → decode, with noise in between. Acknowledgement is not understanding: ask the receiver to summarize back, get a commitment to act, and treat silence as a signal.
- **Transparency:** information radiators, open backlogs and iteration reviews. Distributed teams lose osmotic communication and need live time in overlap hours. Hybrid projects need joint integration reviews.
- **Feedback loops:** arrange reviews around the people whose feedback matters; give representative users hands-on access to increments if they cannot attend. Measure effectiveness by understanding and outcomes (interviews, issue log), not reports sent.
- **Reporting obligations** come from contracts, organizational governance (PMO) and regulators. Confirm a new body's requirements before reusing a report; consolidate overlaps from one data set; tailor a mismatched template with its owner.
- **Aligned reports:** start from the decisions each audience makes; one source of truth, several views; report outcomes against the business case, not just activity.
- **Governance tolerances:** absorb variance inside your delegated limit; escalate a breach (or a forecast breach where the rule covers forecasts) with the recovery plan. Never send a "watermelon" report; correct a known error before decisions rest on it.
- **Agile forecast for governance:** remaining points ÷ velocity = iterations; iterations × cost per iteration = remaining cost (120 ÷ 30 = 4 iterations; at $50,000 each, $200,000).

**Traps**

- The communications plan holds the escalation process; the stakeholder engagement plan holds engagement strategies.
- Email open rates and on-time reports measure activity, not effectiveness.
- A recovery plan does not remove the duty to escalate a breach.
